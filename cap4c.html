4.3. Plume Formation

The numerical simulation of droplets is highly complex when using VoF methods, even with an adaptive mesh refinement approach. This is due to the mesh resolution required to accurately capture the interface. The smallest droplets undergo no significant deformation and have a sphericity close to 1. This means that these droplets can be approximated as spherical particles and therefore resolved using a Lagrangian method. The advantage of a Lagrangian method over VoF is that it requires significantly less computational power and considerably reduces the calculation time. This model is referred to as EHD-VoF-LPT because it combines the EHD model with the VoF method and subsequently couples it with the LPT model.

In our investigations, we present a hybrid model based on the three-dimensional coupling of a hydrodynamic VoF model with a Lagrangian particle-tracking model (Heinrich, 2020). The electric-field equations, derived from the reduced form of Maxwell's equations for an electrostatic field, are added to this algorithm. The Maxwell Stress Tensor (MST) is also incorporated into the momentum equations governing the continuous-phase flow field.
