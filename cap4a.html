4.1. Axisymmetric Jets

The initial studies in this work focus on electrohydrodynamic jets that are radially stable. This means that the objective is to calculate the flow of axisymmetric Taylor cone-jets. This type of jet forms when the inlet flow-rate conditions and the applied electric potential lie within a radially stable operating window. Therefore, the only liquid-jet instability considered is caused by surface tension rather than bending. In this context, the axisymmetric jet case refers to the computational modelling of the experimental tests reported in [30], whose exact operating conditions are reproduced numerically. This case was selected because it enables validation of the electrohydrodynamic model for jet breakup through direct comparison with scaling laws for the diameter of the generated droplets.
